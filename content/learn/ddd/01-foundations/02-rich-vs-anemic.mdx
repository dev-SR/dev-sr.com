---
title: 'Rich vs anemic'
description: 'Where business rules live — and why that choice matters.'
---

## Rich domain model

Entities **own behavior and invariants**:

- `Customer` validates identity/name and owns rentals safely
- `Equipment` reserves/releases itself
- `Rental` creates itself with valid dates and computes its fee

Callers ask the domain to do work; they do not poke fields and hope.

## Anemic domain model

Entities are **data bags** (getters/setters). A service:

1. loads entities
2. checks every rule
3. mutates fields
4. saves

That works for simple CRUD. It fails when rules grow: the service becomes a god object, rules scatter, and invalid states are easy to construct.

## Symptom → remedy

| Symptom | Remedy |
| --- | --- |
| Empty entities | Move decisions onto the entity |
| Service knows all rules | Keep orchestration thin; domain owns checks |
| Public setters | Expose behavior methods |
| Leaky collections | Private list + `IReadOnlyCollection` |
